The Prison Years Of Nelson Mandela

Nelson Mandela was arrested in 1962 and later sentenced to life imprisonment in the Rivonia Trial in 1964. He spent many years in prison because of his involvement in the struggle against apartheid.

Robben Island

Mandela spent most of his imprisonment at Robben Island. Prisoners were subjected to difficult conditions and hard labour. Despite this, Mandela continued studying, communicating with other prisoners, and maintaining his commitment to the struggle.

Life In Prison

Mandela spent 18 years on Robben Island before being transferred to other prisons. During his imprisonment, he became an important symbol of resistance to apartheid. His determination and continued political influence attracted international attention.

Victor Verster Prison

In 1988, Mandela was moved to Victor Verster Prison, where he spent his final period of imprisonment. During this time, discussions between Mandela and representatives of the South African government helped create a path toward negotiations.

27 Years In Prison

Mandela spent a total of 27 years in prison. On February 11, 1990, he was released by President F. W. de Klerk.

A Turning Point

Mandela's release marked an important moment in South Africa's history. After his release, he continued working toward negotiations to end apartheid and establish a democratic South Africa.

Closing

Mandela's years in prison became an important part of his story. His release in 1990 began a new chapter in his journey toward South Africa's transition to democracy.
