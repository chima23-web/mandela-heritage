The life of Nelson Mandela

Becoming President

Nelson Mandela became South Africa's first democratically elected president on 10 May 1994, following the country's first democratic elections. His election marked a major turning point in South Africa's history and the beginning of a new democratic era.

National Unity

As president, Mandela focused strongly on national unity and reconciliation. He encouraged South Africans from different racial and political backgrounds to work together after decades of apartheid and division.

Rebuilding South Africa

Mandela's government worked to improve people's lives by expanding access to housing, education, healthcare, and basic services. His administration also began addressing some of the inequalities created by apartheid.

Truth and Reconciliation

Mandela supported the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, which investigated serious human-rights abuses committed during the apartheid era. The commission gave victims an opportunity to tell their stories and helped the country confront its difficult past.

The 1995 Rugby World Cup

During the 1995 Rugby World Cup, Mandela famously wore the Springbok jersey when he presented the trophy to South Africa's winning team. The moment became a powerful symbol of his efforts to promote unity among South Africans.

Leaving Office

Mandela served one five-year term and stepped down as president in 1999, handing the presidency to Thabo Mbeki. His presidency helped establish South Africa's new democratic government and began a long process of reconciliation and rebuilding.
