The life of Nelson Mandela

The Struggle of Nelson Mandela

Nelson Mandela became increasingly involved in the struggle against apartheid after joining the African National Congress (ANC) in 1944. He helped establish the ANC Youth League, which pushed for stronger resistance to racial discrimination and inequality.

The Defiance Campaign

In 1952, Mandela became National Volunteer-in-Chief of the Defiance Campaign. The campaign used organized civil disobedience to challenge apartheid laws. Mandela and other participants were arrested and later received suspended sentences.

The Treason Trial

In 1956, Mandela and many other anti-apartheid activists were arrested and charged with treason. The trial lasted several years, but Mandela and the remaining defendants were eventually acquitted in 1961.

Sharpeville and the ANC Ban

In 1960, police killed 69 people during a protest at Sharpeville. Following the event, the South African government banned the ANC. Mandela then went underground as the struggle entered a new phase.

A New Phase of the Struggle

In 1961, Mandela helped establish Umkhonto weSizwe, the armed wing of the ANC, and became its first commander-in-chief. In 1962, he secretly left South Africa for military training and to seek support for the struggle. He returned later that year and was arrested.

The Rivonia Trial

In 1963–1964, Mandela and other activists were tried for sabotage in what became known as the Rivonia Trial. In June 1964, Mandela and seven other defendants were sentenced to life imprisonment.

Closing

Mandela's struggle against apartheid eventually led to more than 27 years of imprisonment, but his political journey continued. His release in 1990 marked a major turning point in South Africa's transition toward democracy.
