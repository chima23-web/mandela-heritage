The life of Nelson Mandela

The freedom of Nelson Mandela

After 27 years in prison, Nelson Mandela was released on February 11, 1990. His release became an important moment in South Africa's journey away from apartheid.

Mandela's Release

Mandela walked out of Victor Verster Prison and addressed supporters and journalists. He reaffirmed his commitment to ending apartheid and working toward a democratic South Africa.

The Road To Democracy

After his release, Mandela became involved in negotiations with the South African government. These discussions helped prepare the country for major political changes and the end of apartheid.

The End Of Apartheid

In the early 1990s, South Africa began dismantling apartheid laws and preparing for democratic elections. Mandela and other leaders worked toward a peaceful political transition.

The 1994 Election

In April 1994, South Africa held its first democratic election in which people of all racial groups could vote. Mandela was elected president and was inaugurated on May 10, 1994.

A New South Africa

As president, Mandela promoted reconciliation and encouraged South Africans to work together to build a democratic nation.

Closing

Mandela's release from prison marked the beginning of a new chapter. His journey from prisoner to president became an important part of South Africa's transition to democracy.
